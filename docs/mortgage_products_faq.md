## General Mortgage Questions

**Q: What is a mortgage?**
A mortgage is a loan that allows you to purchase a home using the property itself as collateral. You borrow a sum of money from a lender, agree to repay it over a set period (the loan term) with interest, and the lender holds a lien on the property until the loan is repaid in full. If you fail to make payments, the lender has the right to foreclose and take ownership of the property.

**Q: What is the difference between a fixed-rate and an adjustable-rate mortgage?**
A fixed-rate mortgage has an interest rate that remains the same for the entire life of the loan. Your principal and interest payment never changes, making budgeting predictable. An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) has an interest rate that is fixed for an initial period (e.g., 5 years on a 5/1 ARM) and then adjusts periodically — typically annually — based on a market index plus a margin. ARMs often start with lower rates than fixed loans but carry the risk of payment increases over time.

**Q: What does the notation "5/1 ARM" mean?**
The first number (5) indicates how many years the initial rate is fixed. The second number (1) indicates how often the rate adjusts after the fixed period — in this case, once per year. A 7/1 ARM has a 7-year fixed period followed by annual adjustments. A 10/1 ARM has a 10-year fixed period. Most ARMs also have rate caps that limit how much the rate can increase per adjustment and over the life of the loan.

**Q: What are ARM rate caps?**
Rate caps protect borrowers from extreme payment increases on adjustable-rate mortgages. There are typically three caps expressed as a set of numbers (e.g., 2/2/5):
- **Initial cap**: The maximum rate increase at the first adjustment (e.g., 2% above the initial rate)
- **Periodic cap**: The maximum rate increase at each subsequent adjustment (e.g., 2% per year)
- **Lifetime cap**: The maximum rate increase over the life of the loan (e.g., 5% above the initial rate)

## Loan Types

**Q: What is a conforming loan?**
A conforming loan meets the purchase guidelines established by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, including loan amount limits set annually by the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA). For 2025, the conforming loan limit for a single-family property in most U.S. counties is $766,550. Loans above this limit are called jumbo loans and are not eligible for purchase by the GSEs.

**Q: What is a jumbo loan?**
A jumbo loan is a mortgage that exceeds the conforming loan limits set by the FHFA. Because jumbo loans cannot be sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, lenders take on more risk and typically require stricter qualification standards — higher credit scores (700+ at Meridian Bank), lower DTI ratios (43% maximum), larger down payments (minimum 20%), and substantial cash reserves (12+ months PITIA).

**Q: What is an FHA loan?**
An FHA loan is insured by the Federal Housing Administration, a division of the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). FHA insurance protects the lender against borrower default. Because of this insurance, FHA loans allow for lower credit scores (as low as 500 with 10% down, or 580 with 3.5% down) and higher debt-to-income ratios than conventional loans. FHA loans require both an upfront mortgage insurance premium (UFMIP) of 1.75% of the loan amount and an annual mortgage insurance premium (MIP) for the life of the loan in most cases.

**Q: What is a VA loan?**
A VA loan is guaranteed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs and is available to eligible veterans, active-duty service members, and surviving spouses. VA loans offer significant benefits including no down payment requirement, no private mortgage insurance, competitive interest rates, and limited closing costs. Meridian Bank applies a minimum credit score overlay of 580 for VA loans. An upfront VA Funding Fee is required (varies by down payment, service type, and whether it is the borrower's first VA loan).

**Q: What is a USDA loan?**
A USDA Rural Development loan is backed by the U.S. Department of Agriculture and is designed for borrowers purchasing homes in eligible rural and suburban areas. Like VA loans, USDA loans require no down payment. Borrowers must meet income limits (generally 115% of area median income) and the property must be in a USDA-eligible location. Meridian Bank requires a minimum 640 credit score for USDA loans.

## Qualification and Eligibility

**Q: What is debt-to-income (DTI) ratio and why does it matter?**
DTI ratio compares your total monthly debt obligations to your gross monthly income. Lenders use it to assess whether you can comfortably afford the new mortgage payment on top of your existing debts. There are two DTI measures:
- **Front-end (housing) DTI**: Your proposed housing payment (PITIA) divided by gross monthly income. Conventional guideline: 28%.
- **Back-end (total) DTI**: All monthly debt payments (housing + car loans + credit cards + student loans, etc.) divided by gross monthly income. Conventional guideline: 43%.

**Q: What is loan-to-value (LTV) ratio?**
LTV is the loan amount divided by the appraised value (or purchase price, whichever is lower) of the property, expressed as a percentage. A $200,000 loan on a $250,000 home has an LTV of 80%. Higher LTV means less equity and more risk for the lender. LTV affects your rate (higher LTV typically means higher rate), PMI requirements, and loan eligibility.

**Q: What credit score do I need to get a mortgage?**
Minimum credit score requirements vary by loan type. At Meridian Bank, conventional conforming loans for a primary residence require a minimum 620 FICO score, though borrowers below 680 face additional DTI restrictions. FHA loans are available at 580 (3.5% down) or 500 (10% down). VA and USDA loans have a 580 and 640 minimum respectively. Jumbo loans require a minimum 700. Higher scores generally result in better rates and terms.

**Q: What is the minimum down payment required?**
Minimum down payments depend on the loan type:
- Conventional conforming (primary residence, 1-unit): as low as 3% (97% LTV)
- FHA: 3.5% (with 580+ credit score) or 10% (with 500-579 credit score)
- VA: 0% (no down payment required for eligible borrowers)
- USDA: 0% (no down payment required in eligible areas)
- Conventional jumbo: 20% minimum at Meridian Bank

**Q: Can I use gift funds for my down payment?**
Yes, gift funds from a family member are an acceptable source for down payment and closing costs on most loan types. The gift must be documented with a gift letter stating the donor's name, relationship to borrower, amount, property address, and confirmation that no repayment is required. Bank statements showing the transfer of funds are also required. Gift funds from interested parties in the transaction (seller, agent, builder) are subject to strict limits.

## Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI)

**Q: What is PMI and when is it required?**
Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) is insurance that protects the lender (not the borrower) if you stop making payments. PMI is required on conventional loans when the LTV exceeds 80% — in other words, when your down payment is less than 20%. FHA loans have their own mortgage insurance (MIP) that functions similarly. VA and USDA loans do not require monthly PMI but have their own upfront guarantee fees.

**Q: How much does PMI cost?**
PMI premiums typically range from 0.5% to 1.5% of the original loan amount per year, depending on your credit score, LTV ratio, and loan term. For example, on a $300,000 loan with a 0.8% PMI rate, the annual PMI cost would be $2,400, or $200 per month added to your mortgage payment.

**Q: When can I cancel PMI?**
Under the Homeowners Protection Act (HPA) of 1998:
- You can **request** PMI cancellation when your LTV reaches 80% based on the original amortization schedule or when you have made additional principal payments to reach 80% LTV, provided your payment history is satisfactory and there are no subordinate liens.
- PMI must be **automatically terminated** when your LTV reaches 78% based on the original amortization schedule, provided payments are current.
- For FHA loans originated after June 2013 with less than 10% down, MIP is required for the life of the loan and cannot be cancelled except by refinancing.

## Escrow Accounts

**Q: What is an escrow account?**
An escrow account (also called an impound account) is a separate account held by your lender or loan servicer to collect and hold funds for property taxes and homeowners insurance. Each month, in addition to your principal and interest payment, you pay one-twelfth of your estimated annual property taxes and insurance premiums into escrow. When those bills come due, the servicer pays them on your behalf from the escrow account.

**Q: Is an escrow account required?**
Escrow accounts are required on all FHA, VA, and USDA loans. For conventional loans, escrow is typically required when your LTV exceeds 80% (i.e., when PMI is required). Borrowers with conventional loans at 80% LTV or below may sometimes waive escrow, though lenders may charge an escrow waiver fee of 0.125% to 0.25% of the loan amount.

**Q: What is an escrow analysis?**
Lenders are required by RESPA to perform an annual escrow analysis to ensure the escrow account has sufficient funds. If the analysis shows a shortage (actual expenses exceeded projections), you will receive a notice with options to pay the shortage in a lump sum or spread it over the next 12 months with a higher monthly payment. If there is a surplus exceeding $50, the servicer must refund it.

## Closing Costs and Loan Estimates

**Q: What are closing costs?**
Closing costs are fees and expenses paid at loan closing, in addition to the down payment. They typically range from 2% to 5% of the loan amount and may include: origination charges, appraisal fee, title search and title insurance, attorney fees (in attorney states), prepaid interest, homeowners insurance premium, property tax escrow deposits, recording fees, and transfer taxes.

**Q: What is a Loan Estimate?**
A Loan Estimate (LE) is a three-page standardized form that lenders are required by federal law (TRID/Know Before You Owe) to provide within three business days of receiving a complete loan application. It shows the key loan terms — loan amount, interest rate, monthly payment, and projected closing costs — so borrowers can compare offers from multiple lenders.

**Q: What is a Closing Disclosure?**
A Closing Disclosure (CD) is a five-page standardized form that lenders must provide at least three business days before closing. It shows the final, actual loan terms and closing costs. Borrowers should compare the CD carefully to their Loan Estimate to identify any material changes. Certain fees cannot increase from the LE to the CD (zero tolerance items), while others may increase by up to 10% (limited tolerance items).

## Refinancing

**Q: What is the difference between a rate/term refinance and a cash-out refinance?**
A rate/term refinance replaces your existing mortgage with a new one to get a better interest rate, change the loan term, or switch from an ARM to a fixed rate. No cash is taken out beyond what is needed to cover closing costs. A cash-out refinance replaces your existing mortgage with a larger loan, allowing you to receive the difference in cash. The cash-out amount is limited by your home's equity and the lender's maximum LTV for cash-out refinances (80% LTV maximum at Meridian Bank for conventional primary residences).

**Q: How do I know if refinancing makes financial sense?**
A basic break-even analysis: divide your total closing costs by your monthly savings to determine how many months it takes to recoup the cost. For example, if refinancing costs $4,000 and saves $150/month, the break-even point is approximately 27 months. If you plan to stay in the home longer than the break-even period, refinancing likely makes financial sense. Also consider whether you are restarting a 30-year term and the total interest paid over the new loan's life.

**Q: What is a prepayment penalty?**
A prepayment penalty is a fee charged by some lenders if you pay off your mortgage early — either through refinancing or selling the home — before a specified period, typically 3-5 years. Prepayment penalties on qualified mortgages (QMs) are restricted by the CFPB's Ability-to-Repay/Qualified Mortgage rule. Meridian Bank does not charge prepayment penalties on any of its standard residential mortgage products.

## The Loan Process

**Q: What is the difference between pre-qualification and pre-approval?**
Pre-qualification is an informal estimate of how much you might be able to borrow, based on self-reported information provided without verification. It carries no commitment from the lender. Pre-approval is a more formal process in which the lender verifies your income, assets, and credit through documentation review and a hard credit pull. A pre-approval letter carries more weight with sellers and is generally valid for 60-90 days.

**Q: How long does the mortgage process take?**
From application to closing, the mortgage process typically takes 30 to 45 days, though this can vary. Key stages include: application and document collection (1-5 days), processing (5-10 days), appraisal (7-14 days), underwriting review (5-10 business days), conditional approval and condition clearing (3-7 days), and closing disclosure delivery and closing (3 business days after CD delivery, minimum).

**Q: What documents will I need to apply?**
Typical documentation required includes: government-issued photo ID; Social Security number; 30 days of recent pay stubs; W-2s for the past 2 years; federal tax returns for the past 2 years (signed); 2-3 months of bank and investment account statements; most recent mortgage statement (if applicable); homeowners insurance information; and for self-employed borrowers, business tax returns and year-to-date P&L.
